TurkicNumerals and Pronouns

Numbers, pronouns and the sounds behind them

A closer look at one family. The numerals and pronouns of the Turkic languages are set side by side, split into syllables and traced back through a set of proposed sound shifts to an Early Turkic layer. The Early Turkic forms and the shifts are this site’s working reconstruction: hypothetical, and open to revision.

Sound shifts

In Turkish, vi / hi / ha develop into ü and ö: when a laryngeal (throat) consonant is swallowed, it turns the next vowel round.

  • Visse→Üç
  • Tahatta→Dört
  1. hi / ha→ü, öhi / ha → ü, ö
  2. kh→y→jkh → y → j
  3. ğ→h?ğ → h
  4. d→l→şd → l → ş
  5. t→r→st → r → s
  6. m→nm → n
  7. h→y→sh → y → s

Numeral by numeral

Each Early Turkic form is split into syllables. The notes show which of the shifts above carry it into Turkish and Chuvash.

1*berre

TurkishbirChuvashpirre

  • -re is lost in Turkish; Chuvash keeps it (pir-re).
  • Chuvash p- for b-.
2*ğekke

TurkishikiChuvashikke

  • ğ → h → ∅: the initial throat sound is lost.
  • -ke weakens to -i in Turkish; Chuvash keeps -ke.
3*herre

TurkishüçChuvashvişşe

  • hi / he → ü: the laryngeal is swallowed and rounds the vowel.
  • Chuvash v- keeps a trace of the lost onset (vi → ü).
4*dahatta

TurkishdörtChuvashtavatta

  • ha → ö: -dah- becomes dö-.
  • t → r: -tt- becomes -rt.
5*beddeh

TurkishbeşChuvashpillek

  • d → l → ş: Turkish goes to ş, Chuvash stops at l (pillek).
6*yadta

TurkishaltıChuvashultta

  • y- is lost before a; d → l gives al-.
  • -ta is the suffix (Turkish -tı, Chuvash -tta).
7*yette

TurkishyediChuvashsiçe

  • kh → y: Turkish keeps y-.
  • h → y → s: Chuvash goes further to s- (siçe).
8*yeker

TurkishsekizChuvashsakkar

  • y → s at the start (h → y → s).
  • “two short”: yek / hek / sek is “two”, -er the “lacking” suffix.
9*tahar

TurkishdokuzChuvashtahhar

  • ha → o: the laryngeal rounds the vowel (doh- → do-).
  • “one short”: tah “one, single”, -ar “lacking”. Chuvash keeps h (tahhar).
10*hun

TurkishonChuvashvunna

  • hu → o: the initial h is swallowed before a round vowel.
  • Chuvash v- again marks the lost onset (vunna).

Pronouns

The personal pronouns follow the same pattern across the family: m- or b- for the first person, s- for the second. Chuvash e- forms (epĕ, esĕ) have lost the initial consonant.

NoteThe closest relative

On this site’s view, the family closest to Turkic, in vocabulary as well as in structure, is Uralic. The two share the whole grammatical profile listed below, and a run of basic words, including most numerals from two to seven and the personal pronouns. Most linguists today read these likenesses as typology or contact rather than proof of a common ancestor (the older Ural-Altaic theory is no longer widely held); the comparison here argues that the overlap is too regular to be chance.

Shared structure

  • Agglutination: words are built by adding suffixes one after another.
  • Vowel harmony: suffix vowels follow the vowels of the stem.
  • Subject–object–verb order, with modifiers before the noun.
  • Postpositions instead of prepositions.
  • No grammatical gender, and no definite article.
  • Possession marked with personal suffixes on the noun.
  • A numeral is followed by a singular noun (iki göz, “two eye”).

And Basque?

Basque, a language isolate in the western Pyrenees, shows a number of possible likenesses with both: aita / ata “father”, ama / ana “mother”, ni / men “I”, zu / sen “you”, and several numerals. If they are more than chance, three explanations are open: a deep layer shared by all three, ancient contact along the steppe and the Caucasus, or later borrowing. The table places the working Proto Basque, Basque–Turkic and Proto Turkic forms between the attested words. This is the most speculative part of the page.
