Numerals 1–10

Cognate sets for the numerals 1–7 and 10 across eight language families, and two notes on how 8 and 9 are counted down from ten. Forms are transcribed in IPA; an asterisk marks a reconstructed form. Every Nostratic root and sound path on this page is hypothetical, marked with a question mark, and open to revision as the comparison develops.

8

Numeral 8: Eight, counted down from ten

Counting by subtraction

No shared Nostratic form for 8 is set out here. Several languages build it by subtraction instead: “two short of ten”. The same idea reappears one decade up, where 18 is “two from twenty”.

  • FinnishUralic

    kahdeksan

    kah- “two” + -deksa-, often read as “ten”: two (short of) ten.

  • Northern SamiUralic

    gávcci

    Same formation as Finnish, shortened.

  • Meadow MariUralic

    kandaše

    ka- “two” + -daše, the same “ten” element.

  • MansiUralic

    ńololow

    Contains low “ten”.

  • LatinIndo-Anatolian

    duodēvīgintī

    duo “two” + dē “from” + vīgintī “twenty”.

    18 = 20 − 2

  • EtruscanTyrsenian

    eslem zaθrum

    esl- “two” + -em “from” + zaθrum “twenty”.

    18 = 20 − 2

9

Numeral 9: Nine, counted down from ten

Counting by subtraction

Nine follows the same pattern as “one short of ten”, and the decade numbers show it most clearly.

  • FinnishUralic

    yhdeksän

    yh- “one” + -deksä-: one (short of) ten.

  • Northern SamiUralic

    ovcci

    Same formation as Finnish.

  • Meadow MariUralic

    indeše

    The same “ten” element as kandaše.

  • MansiUralic

    ontolow

    Contains low “ten”.

  • LatinIndo-Anatolian

    ūndēvīgintī

    ūnus “one” + dē “from” + vīgintī “twenty”.

    19 = 20 − 1

  • SanskritIndo-Anatolian

    ūnaviṃśati

    ūna “lacking” + viṃśati “twenty”.

    19 = 20 − 1

  • Ancient GreekIndo-Anatolian

    ἑνὸς δέοντες εἴκοσι

    “twenty lacking one”.

    19 = 20 − 1

  • EtruscanTyrsenian

    θunem cialχ

    θu- “one” + -em “from” + cialχ “thirty”.

    29 = 30 − 1
