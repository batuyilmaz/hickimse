μῆτις

Nobody got out.

Μῆτις means cunning, and it rhymes with μή τις, “not anyone”. The name was the trick. Out at sea, Odysseus could not keep it to himself:

“Cyclops, if any one asks you who it was that put your eye out and spoiled your beauty, say it was the valiant warrior Ulysses, son of Laertes, who lives in Ithaca.”

Odyssey 9.502–505, translated by Samuel Butler (1900).

And so Nobody got a name back, and the gods heard it. Some secrets are safer kept.
